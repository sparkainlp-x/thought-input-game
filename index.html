How a drawn thought becomes a hologram and a sound

The same four steps as our membrane toys — heart, brain, propulsion ring — now driven by your input instead of a built-in signal.

  1. 01

    You think in a pattern

    No mind-reading: a “thought” enters as a 12 × 12 pattern of lit cells that you draw. The translation from idea to pattern is yours — that translation is the input.

  2. 02

    The matrix remembers by superposition

    Stored thoughts are added together into one shared field, like exposures on one holographic plate. Nothing sits in its own slot — which is why a fragment can rebuild a whole, and why wholes can blur into each other.

  3. 03

    Projection completes — or the gate refuses

    Your probe is compared against every stored thought at once. The strongest match is projected back whole. If nothing reaches the threshold τ, the gate refuses rather than inventing an answer.

  4. 04

    You hear the thought

    The recalled pattern is sonified row by row: each lit cell plays a note set by its column, so a shape you can see becomes a melody you can hear. Same pattern, two senses — vision and sound.

Honest limits. This game reads no minds and no brains: “thought input” means a pattern you deliberately draw. “Holographic” is a metaphor for the memory model: your probe is compared with every stored pattern at once and the matches are combined into one weighted field, so a fragment can recall a whole. The code keeps a list of patterns; it is not a trained weight matrix, and the projection is drawn as a hologram-style picture. It is not an optical hologram, not a brain–computer interface, and not a medical anything. The sounds are a sonification mapping we chose (column → pitch, row → time). Everything runs in this tab; nothing you draw is saved or sent anywhere, and the page loads no outside fonts or scripts.
